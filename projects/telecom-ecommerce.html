Sunrise — Telecom E-commerce

At Infosys, senior frontend on Sunrise’s storefront rebuild — AEM and Angular moved to Storyblok and Next.js, with a micro-frontend split between product discovery and checkout.

ROLESenior Software Developer
COMPANYInfosys
CLIENTSunrise
SCOPEMigration · Micro-frontends

Overview

Sunrise is a Swiss telecom operator — mobile, fibre internet, TV, and devices. Its e-commerce site sold phones, laptops, and subscription products: internet, prepaid, and postpaid plans. The stack was aging on AEM and Angular. The rebuild moved content to Storyblok and the storefront to Next.js, with a micro-frontend split so product and checkout could scale and ship independently. I worked as a senior software developer at Infosys, focused on internet subscription products.

Two microsites: one for the product landing page and listings, one for cart summary, checkout, and order submission. A user picks a plan, lands on summary to adjust the order, then checkout — personal details, identity, address, phone. Submit does not take payment. It creates a lead, emails the customer and Sunrise, and the operator runs a credit check before provisioning.

Product: sunrise.ch

What I Built

  • Product listing / landing for internet subscriptions — PageHeroHeader, accordions, and a product table so users could compare and choose a plan.
  • Cart behaviour that carried a selected product into summary, where the order could still be changed.
  • Checkout form work including file upload and a signature pad for identity and order details.
  • The order confirmation page after lead submission.

Approach

Day to day I shipped my stories, mentored juniors on theirs, estimated work, and joined sprint planning, client grooming, and solution-design sessions with architects. This was also the first time I led a team at Infosys. The first sprint was hard to balance with my own tickets — I kept a written split of tasks and tracked progress daily so neither the team nor delivery slipped.

Two technical stretches sat on top of that. Storyblok was new to me; I had to learn a headless CMS and wire it into Next.js on a short clock, while still contributing. Micro-frontends were built from scratch so the listing site and the cart/checkout site could stay independently scalable. Agile/Scrum was the delivery rhythm throughout.
