Life & Annuity Claims Servicing

At EPAM Systems, built a greenfield frontend for a life-and-annuity servicing platform — 100+ paper claim forms moved online, with policy search, claim-type routing, and dynamic forms on retained legacy services.

ROLEFrontend · occasional lead
COMPANYEPAM Systems
DOMAINLife & annuity
SCOPEGreenfield UI · ~2 years

Overview

Life and annuity in-force work still ran on paper and disconnected ops — claims, withdrawals, and policy changes that should have been one digital workflow for policyholders and the operations team. At EPAM Systems I spent about two years on the frontend that closed that gap: a modern, secure, accessible portal on top of retained legacy services, so the book of business did not have to wait for a core rewrite.

After a secure login, a user searches for a policy. Claim options depend on policy type — death, maturity, partial withdrawal, or full withdrawal — then a dynamic form for that path. More than 100 legacy paper forms moved onto that surface. New behavior had to ship behind flags, in multiple languages, without breaking production.

What I Built

  • Claim-type business logic and dynamic forms driven by policy data and user selection — death, maturity, and withdrawal paths, not just static screens.
  • Tab-based navigation so multiple in-force claim workflows could live in one layout.
  • A data-heavy table with offset-based pagination (React Query) for large policy and claim lists.
  • Reusable UI (accordions, progress bars, and other primitives) plus design-system work so 100+ form surfaces stayed consistent.
  • Validation and error handling across claim scenarios so submit paths failed clearly, not silently.
  • Feature flags and i18n so new servicing behavior could roll out safely across locales.
  • Accessibility work that moved compliance from 63% to 87%, with semantic HTML and ARIA on dynamic content.
  • Logging tuned so production defects were easier to find and hotfix — enough signal, not a flood of noise.

Approach

I mixed hands-on frontend work with occasional leadership. As an individual contributor I owned claim flows, dynamic forms, and the shared UI. As a lead I ran reviews, mentoring, estimates, and interviews, and sat with Product Owners, architects, backend, and QA on how those flows should land — data contracts, integrity, security, and accessibility — so servicing transactions were designed end to end, not painted on after the APIs existed.

I worked with designers on the UX, kept the design system and docs current for onboarding, explained technical choices to non-technical stakeholders, and helped coordinate larger refactors. Knowledge spread through pair programming and internal talks — not only through tickets.
